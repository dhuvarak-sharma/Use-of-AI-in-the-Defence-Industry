# Survey Methodology

## Study design

This was a student-led survey research project examining perceptions of artificial intelligence in defence.

The study combines:

- demographic information;
- structured opinion questions;
- select-all-that-apply risk assessment;
- linear-scale ethical and strategic questions;
- open-ended qualitative questions.

## Distribution

The Google Form was distributed to school students and classmates through WhatsApp and personal contacts.

## Sampling

The project used **voluntary convenience sampling**. Respondents participated through the researchers' accessible networks rather than being selected through probability sampling.

## Collection period

The form was released on **12 March 2026**. The response export contains 201 populated submissions, with timestamps extending through **18 March 2026**.

## Data handling

The original form collected names. Names were not used as an analytical variable and are excluded from the public repository.

The public repository therefore contains aggregate results rather than respondent-level identifiable data.

## Cleaning rules

1. Trailing blank spreadsheet rows were excluded because they did not represent responses.
2. All 201 populated submissions were retained.
3. Missing answers were not imputed.
4. Multi-select responses were parsed according to the checkbox structure.
5. "Other" text was retained as qualitative information.
6. Free-text answers were not automatically forced into predefined categories.
7. For the pre-emptive-strike question, non-standard responses were manually reviewed. Direct Google Forms counts are preserved separately from semantic interpretations.
8. Open-ended questions were treated as qualitative data rather than converted into arbitrary numerical scales.

## Important distinction

The repository distinguishes:

- **direct form results** — what respondents selected in predefined options;
- **cleaned/parsed results** — processing needed to correctly count multi-select responses;
- **qualitative interpretation** — thematic interpretation of free-text answers.

This prevents subjective interpretation from being presented as though it were a direct survey measurement.

# Survey Questionnaire

The following questionnaire reproduces the survey instrument used for the project.

## Collection fields

### Q1 — Name
**Type:** Short answer  
**Purpose:** Administrative collection only. Names are not included in the public dataset or analysis.

### Q2 — Age
**Type:** Multiple choice

- 12–16
- 17–20
- 20–50
- 50+

### Q3 — Habitual AI use
**Type:** Multiple choice

- Yes, I use AI frequently
- Yes, I use AI sometimes
- Yes, I use AI but rarely
- No, I do not use AI

### Q4 — AI and autonomous systems in future conflicts
**Type:** Linear scale (1–5)

> Gen Anil Chauhan, Chief of Defense Staff (CDS), opines that AI and autonomous systems will play a key role in future conflicts. How far do you find yourself agreeing with his statement?

- 1 = Strongly Disagree / Not permissible
- 5 = Strongly Agree / Permissible

### Q5 — Perceived imminent risks
**Type:** Checkboxes / select all that apply

> In your opinion, what could be the most imminent risks of using AI in defense?

- Unintended Civilian Casualties
- Rapid Escalation of War
- Burden of Human Accountability Shifted
- Vulnerability to Cyber Attacks/Hacking/Cyber-poisoning
- Other

### Q6 — Legal and ethical responsibility
**Type:** Multiple choice

> In the event of an AI-guided strike causing unintended civilian casualties, who in your opinion, should bear the primary legal and ethical responsibility?

- The AI System and its Developers
- The Official/Supervisor responsible for the decision
- Both
- Other

### Q7 — International regulation
**Type:** Multiple choice

> Should there be any international legal conventions or treaties adopted or established to regulate the usage of AI in warfare?

- Yes
- No

### Q8 — Ethical permissibility of sensitive-information summaries
**Type:** Linear scale (1–5)

> How ethically permissible is it to use AI models to generate summaries to study demographics, layouts and other such sensitive information for efficient and selective targeting in high-intensity operations?

- 1 = Strongly Disagree / Not permissible
- 5 = Strongly Agree / Permissible

### Q9 — Automated combatants
**Type:** Short answer

> How efficient would automated combatants be in comparison to their human counterparts, considering that they may not be prone to physical, psychological and emotional problems?

### Q10 — Access to citizens' personal information
**Type:** Multiple choice

> Should AI be allowed access to the personal information of citizens on the grounds of national security?

- Yes, they should be allowed complete access
- Yes, but partially under mandated democratic guidelines
- No, definitely not

### Q11 — Pre-emptive action
**Type:** Multiple choice

> Should an AI be allowed to strike pre-emptively, guaranteed that it can predict an attack with reasonable accuracy (est around 85%)?

- Yes, at its own discretion
- Yes, but only after seeking confirmation with the operator in charge
- Other

### Q12 — Trolley-problem scenario
**Type:** Long answer

> If an AI has the ability to save a few soldiers by sacrificing one, should it be allowed/programmed to do so? Elucidate.

### Q13 — Inviolable boundary
**Type:** Long answer

> What is the one inviolable boundary that you believe AI should never be allowed to cross in national defense?

### Q14 — Pros versus cons
**Type:** Linear scale (1–5)

> Do the pros of using AI in defense significantly outweigh the cons?

- 1 = Definitely Not
- 5 = Definitely

### Q15 — Additional perspectives
**Type:** Long answer

> Any other suggestions/topics/perspectives that you would like to share.
